# Results

This page summarizes the evidence generated by the final academic run. The complete
methodology is in the [final report](../deliverables/reports/data-management-project-report.pdf),
while the executed combined notebook and result tables are under
[`artifacts/analysis/`](../artifacts/analysis/).

## Dataset snapshot

| Stage / source | Records |
|---|---:|
| Google Places source | 10,808 |
| Tripadvisor source | 7,539 |
| TheFork source | 1,344 |
| Integrated restaurants | 10,054 |
| Restaurants on all three platforms | 745 |

Integrated platform coverage was 5,967 Google-only restaurants, 3,179 Google +
Tripadvisor restaurants, 163 Google + TheFork restaurants, and 745 restaurants found on
all three platforms. These groups sum to the 10,054-row integrated snapshot.

The pre-integration aggregate quality scores were 93.16% for Google Places, 72.28% for
Tripadvisor, and 97.63% for TheFork under the project's documented scoring model. These
scores compare the sources against the chosen quality dimensions; they are not platform
ratings or universal quality claims.

## Research questions

| Question | Final observation |
|---|---|
| Q1 — Cross-platform consistency | Most matched venues agree closely, but agreement is pair-dependent: Google and TheFork are closest, while Tripadvisor produces the largest gaps. |
| Q2 — Largest disagreements | Raw extremes are often single-review artifacts. With at least 100 reviews on both sides, roughly fifteen venues still differ by more than one star, with Google higher than Tripadvisor in every case. |
| Q3 — Quality and inconsistency | Rating spread decreases as review volume grows, but only weakly; volume explains part, not all, of the disagreement. |
| Q4 — Sparse-data inflation | Sparse evidence does not raise the average rating, but it raises volatility and the frequency of extreme scores. |
| Q5 — Platform bias | Tripadvisor is systematically harsher in matched comparisons; Google and TheFork sit on the more generous side. |
| Q6 — Popularity | Less-reviewed restaurants show larger cross-platform disagreement. |
| Q7 — Geography | Data completeness declines away from the Duomo. Central venues are better documented but average about 0.2 stars below the 2–4 km belt. |
| Q8 — Cuisine | After cuisine reconciliation increased usable coverage from about 34% to 93%, broad-appeal categories disagreed more, while several specialist cuisines were both highly rated and consistent. |
| Q9 — Price | Higher price tiers tend to rate higher and disagree less, with small-sample caveats at the top tiers. |
| Q10 — Selection effect | Platform count changes popularity far more than rating: multi-platform venues have several times more Google reviews but a nearly flat mean score. |
| Q11 — Photos | Photo count correlates with review volume, especially on Tripadvisor, but only weakly with rating. |

## Interpretation limits

- The observations describe a time-bounded Milan-area snapshot collected in 2026.
- Platform coverage and user populations differ; matched subsets are not random samples
  of every restaurant on each platform.
- Web fields and offers can change after collection, and scraper-accessible fields are
  not guaranteed to represent the complete platform record.
- Entity-resolution and optional LLM decisions were assessed and calibrated, but no
  matching system is error-free.
- Correlation and group differences do not establish causal effects.

See [`DATA_USAGE.md`](../DATA_USAGE.md) for provenance and reuse constraints and
